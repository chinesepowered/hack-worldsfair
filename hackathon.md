**Strategic read:** the four $100k tracks pay **2× per project** of the four $25k tracks for the same
work (10 slots at $10k vs. 5 slots at $5k). Tempo, Hyperliquid and Zcash are also likely to be far less
crowded than Solana, which is Colosseum's home ecosystem and will draw the bulk of the field. If the
product idea is chain-agnostic, building on a high-pool / low-competition chain is the highest-EV play.

**Also note:** the hackathon is *not* limited to these chains — "every submission is eligible for prizes
regardless of the crypto infrastructure used." The tracks are bonus pools, not gates. Nothing in the rules
forbids a submission from qualifying for **multiple** tracks if it genuinely integrates several chains,
though that is not explicitly promised either. See §12.

**Prize mechanics (rules §15):** prizes are paid to the **Team Leader** only; the winning team may be
required to set up a designated wallet address to receive funds. Values are stated before taxes; winners
handle their own tax reporting. Prizes are non-transferable.

## 5. Eligibility

**You qualify if:**
- You are **18+** or the age of majority in your country of residence, whichever is older, as of the start
  of the contest. Under 18? Email hello@colosseum.com — decided case by case.
- You are building a **new startup that hasn't raised significant outside capital**. Established companies
  that have been building the same product for years on venture funding are out of scope.
- Non-technical founders are welcome ("We have backed non-technical founders in our Accelerator who built
  MVPs entirely with AI coding tools").

**You are excluded if (rules §3b):**
- You are located in or ordinarily resident in: Afghanistan, Belarus, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia,
  Somalia, Syria, the Crimea/Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson regions of Ukraine,
  Venezuela, or Yemen — or any jurisdiction where participation is prohibited.
- You are subject to US or other applicable sanctions (e.g. OFAC), or employed by a sanctioned entity.
- You are an employee, contractor, director or officer of Colosseum or a Contest Sponsor, or immediate
  family of one.

You also warrant that participating doesn't violate any employer policy or contractual obligation.

## 7. Prior work and pre-existing code — read carefully

This is the rule most likely to disqualify someone:

> "Teams may begin development before the hackathon, but **products are judged only on the work completed
> between the competition's start and end dates**."

> "Builders may use pre-existing code, but teams **must disclose all relevant past development work** in
> the submission form."

> "'Pre-existing code' does not refer to open-source code developed by others. We encourage founders to
> compose with existing crypto protocols."

**Penalties for misrepresenting development history:** disqualification, a ban on the individual builders
from future Colosseum hackathons, and revocation of prizes.

**AI coding tools are explicitly fine** — Colosseum has funded founders whose MVPs were built entirely
with them.

**Practical takeaway:** starting from a prior codebase is legal but strictly worse for scoring, because
only the delta counts. A fresh repo with clean commit history dated inside the window is the cleanest
possible story for the "did significant work during the hackathon" check.

## 8. What you must submit

Submitted via the product-submission portal on the platform dashboard (available after joining the
competition). Treat it as **a pitch to a venture fund**, not a hackathon form.

- [ ] Product name and a brief description
- [ ] Which blockchains and tools are integrated
- [ ] All teammates, with background and previous experience
- [ ] Where the team is located
- [ ] A product logo or graphic
- [ ] **GitHub repository link** — open-source encouraged; private repos allowed *if* access is granted to
      `hackathon@colosseum.com` for review
- [ ] **2–3 minute presentation video** — "one of the first resources judges review, so it should be clear,
      concise, and high quality"
- [ ] **Product demo video, max 3 minutes**, explaining how the product works
- [ ] Go-to-market strategy, demand validation, and plans for developing distribution

Optional but recommended: **weekly ~1-minute progress videos** throughout the hackathon.

All content must be **in English** (rules §12a).

### What they look for in the GitHub repo

Want to see:
- That you **did significant work during the hackathon**
- That **you and your team** did the work, not a third party
- That you **prioritized feature development strategically**

Explicitly *not* looking for:
- A particular language or framework
- Specific design patterns, best practices, or code-quality checks

## 9. Judging

### 9.1 FAQ criteria (how the team actually evaluates)

| Criterion | The question being asked |
|---|---|
| **Founder + Market Fit** | Does the team have the right skills and experience for this market, and why is it motivated to solve this problem? |
| **Insight** | Does the team have a unique insight from deep understanding of the problem space? Is there a new technology or trend creating the opportunity? |
| **Product + Execution** | How well does the product work? How does it compare to competitors? How fast is the team shipping and responding to feedback? |
| **Potential Market Size** | How big is the TAM? Already large, or small but growing fast? |
| **Founder Communication** | Are founders communicating the vision clearly and capable of growing the user base? |
| **Viability** | Can this become a scalable, sustainable business? |
| **Traction** | Does it already have demand or revenue? How durable? |

### 9.2 Official rules criteria (rules §8)

(a) **Functionality** — how well does it work, what's the code quality?
(b) **Potential Impact** — TAM, and impact on the broader crypto ecosystem
(c) **Novelty** — how unique is the concept?
(d) **UX** — how well does it use blockchain to create great UX for downstream users?
(e) **Open-source** — is it open-source? How well does it compose with other crypto primitives?
(f) **Business Plan** — is there a viable business here, and can this team execute on the vision?

> Criterion (e) is a direct, scored incentive to **open-source the repo** and to **compose with existing
> protocols** rather than build in isolation.

### 9.3 Process

1. Submissions close
2. Colosseum team runs **multiple rounds of evaluation**
3. Shortlist of highest-quality products advances to the **judging panel**
4. Individual judging narrows further
5. Smallest group invited to a **15-minute Zoom interview**
6. Winners chosen; announced ~1 month after deadline

Not every team gets feedback — volume prevents it — but every submission is reviewed by the Colosseum team
and/or the judging panel. Administrator's decisions are **final and binding**.

  requires advance written approval.

- **Code of Conduct** applies throughout: be respectful, professional, thoughtful (minors may be present),
  and open. Violations can mean disqualification.

## 16. Open questions to resolve

These were not answerable from the published material and are worth confirming in Discord or with
`hackathon@colosseum.com`:

1. **Maximum team size** — never stated anywhere in the rules, FAQ, or site. Assume no hard cap, but confirm
   before building a large team.
2. **Multi-track eligibility** — can one submission win in more than one ecosystem track (e.g. deployed on
   both Solana and Base)? The rules describe each track independently and never prohibit it, but never
   confirm it either. Materially affects chain strategy.
3. **University Award criteria** — no definition of what qualifies a team as "university" (all members
   enrolled? one member? currently enrolled vs. recent grad?).
4. **Public Goods Award criteria** — no published definition; presumably non-commercial / open infrastructure,
   but the standard is unstated.
5. **Additional tracks and workshops** — rules §14 says "new awards may be added in the future," and only
   3 of a likely longer workshop series are currently listed. Re-check the site weekly.
6. **Per-track judging detail** — track judges are named collectively; which judges cover which track is not
   published.
